# Research and sales scripts

Date: 1 September 2026

These scripts are designed to uncover observed behaviour and paid intent. Do not pitch the answer before understanding the last real job search or hire.

## 1. Candidate interview screener

Recruit people who, in the last six months, searched in Berlin for at least one of:

- minijob;
- part-time work;
- working-student work;
- short-term/seasonal work; or
- a second-income job.

Do not recruit only students or only English speakers. Record segment, not protected-characteristic judgments.

## 2. Candidate interview — 25 minutes

### Context

1. Tell me about the last time you looked for flexible or part-time work in Berlin.
2. What else in your week did the job have to fit around?
3. Which sites, apps, groups or people did you actually use, and in what order?

### Walk through the last search

4. Show me the search if you still have it, or reconstruct the exact filters and terms you used.
5. What information did you need before you were willing to apply?
6. Which information was hardest to find or trust?
7. How many jobs looked relevant, and how many did you actually apply to?
8. What made you abandon a listing or application?
9. Did any job turn out to be closed, different from the ad or suspicious? What exactly happened?
10. What response did you receive, and how long did it take?

### Prototype test

11. Using this prototype, find one job you would genuinely apply to. Think aloud.
12. What do you believe “Checked 6h ago” means?
13. Which of wage, schedule, hours, travel time, language or employment type would you never compromise on?
14. Is anything here making a promise you do not believe?
15. Would you apply directly to the employer, or prefer one reusable availability card? Why?

### Commitment

16. May I send you only jobs matching the availability you just gave?
17. Which alert channel would you actually open?
18. Would you use this for your next search? Ask for the next-search date, not a 1–10 rating.

### Evidence to record

- actual products used;
- minutes spent per session;
- applications sent;
- wage/schedule/language/commute requirements;
- dead or suspicious listings;
- response time;
- prototype time to first suitable job;
- apply intent tied to a real job; and
- consented follow-up channel.

## 3. Employer screener

Recruit Berlin employers who hired at least two flexible, hourly, part-time or working-student employees in the last 12 months, or who expect to do so in the next 60 days.

Cover retail, hospitality/events, logistics/fulfilment and office/customer support. Include small single-site and multi-location employers.

## 4. Employer interview — 30 minutes

### Last real hire

1. Tell me about the last flexible or part-time role you tried to fill.
2. What exact days, hours, pay and language level did you need?
3. Where did you publish it and why those channels?
4. What did each channel cost in money and staff time?
5. How many applications, suitable conversations, interviews and hires came from each?
6. What made applications unsuitable?
7. How quickly did you respond, and who owned that work?
8. Did people fail to show up, cancel or leave quickly? What preceded that?
9. How did you close or update the posting when the role changed or filled?

### Data and workflow

10. Which fields could you commit to stating publicly: hourly pay, weekly hours, actual shift windows, language level and contract type?
11. Which of those are genuinely unknown at posting time, and why?
12. Could you reconfirm an open role once a week with one click?
13. Would direct employer applications or a standardized availability card be easier for you?
14. What applicant data do you truly need before a first conversation?

### Paid pilot

15. I can publish this role in a structured, verified format and distribute it to Berlin candidates whose stated availability fits. The 14-day pilot is €79. It does not guarantee a hire. Shall we use the role you just described?

If no:

16. What specifically prevents the purchase: audience proof, timing, budget, authority, terms or the product itself?
17. Who owns the decision and when is the next real hiring need?

Do not convert “interesting” into commercial evidence. Only payment, a signed pilot or a scheduled decision with the actual buyer counts.

## 5. Employer listing intake

Collect:

- legal company name and website;
- authorized contact and domain email;
- role title;
- employment type;
- gross hourly wage or range;
- minimum/maximum weekly hours;
- exact required or likely days/time windows;
- flexibility and notice period;
- location;
- required German level and other working languages;
- start date and expected duration;
- necessary experience/certification;
- accessible workplace information where relevant;
- application destination;
- application materials actually required;
- response owner and target time; and
- confirmation that the role is real, open and authorized for publication.

## 6. Landing-page message test

### Variant A — recommended

> **Berlin jobs that fit your week.**  
> See the pay, real shifts, travel time, language and last live check before you apply.

CTA: **Choose when you can work**

### Variant B — trust-led

> **Only real, live flexible jobs in Berlin.**  
> Verified employers. Visible pay and hours. Dead jobs disappear.

CTA: **See jobs checked today**

### Variant C — category-led control

> **Minijobs, part-time and working-student jobs in Berlin.**

CTA: **Find a job**

Success is qualified search completion and application, not click-through alone.

## 7. Five-second result-card test

Show one card for five seconds, hide it and ask:

1. What is the job?
2. What does it pay?
3. When does it happen?
4. How far away is it?
5. What language is needed?
6. Why did you trust or distrust it?

If candidates cannot answer the first five, the card failed.

## 8. Founding-employer offer

> We are testing a Berlin-only job product for minijob, part-time, working-student and short-term roles. Candidates search by the days and hours they can actually work. Every listing must state pay, schedule, hours, language and contract type, and it disappears when it is no longer live.
>
> The 14-day founding pilot costs €79. It includes job structuring, employer/source verification, matched alert distribution and a results report. It does not guarantee a hire, and payment does not buy a verification badge or bypass ranking quality. If our own delivery system materially fails, we rerun the pilot at no charge.

Ask: **Which real open role should we pilot?**

## 9. Community-partner request

Use only with a relevant administrator or organization contact.

> We are validating a Berlin-only search for flexible jobs that shows pay, actual shifts, travel time, language and when each role was last checked. We would like to interview five members who searched for part-time or minijob work recently. This is research, not a mass mailing, and we will not add anyone to alerts without separate consent. May we share the interview invitation once in your group?

## 10. Debrief scorecard

After every five interviews, record:

| Question | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Which segment has an urgent repeated problem? | |
| Which current workaround is strongest? | |
| Which mandatory field changes application decisions? | |
| Which proposed promise is misunderstood? | |
| Which employer data is unavailable in practice? | |
| What was paid for or formally committed? | |
| What would falsify the current wedge? | |

Do not add features because one person requested them. Promote a finding only after repeated behaviour or a strong economic/safety reason.
